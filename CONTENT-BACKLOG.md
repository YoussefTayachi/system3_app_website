## Wie du damit arbeitest

**Aufnahme in einem Rutsch.** Alle zehn an einem Nachmittag filmen, dasselbe
Hemd, dasselbe Licht. Drei Wochen Vorlauf, und du postest ohne Druck.

**Erste drei Sekunden.** Der Hook steht im ersten Satz UND als Text im Bild.
Nicht anmoderieren. Kein „Hi, ich bin Youssef" — das kostet die Sekunden, in
denen entschieden wird.

**Aufbau immer gleich:** Hook → Beleg → Umkehrung → ein Satz, der hängen
bleibt. Kein „folgt mir für mehr".

**Untertitel sind Pflicht.** Der größere Teil schaut ohne Ton.

## 01 · I sent 2,715 cold emails

**Säule:** Öffentlich bauen · **Länge:** 45 s · **Der Startbeitrag.**

> Er ist unangenehm, er ist wahr, und er macht in dreißig Sekunden glaubwürdig,
> was zehn Ratgeber-Videos nicht schaffen. Wer damit anfängt, kann danach über
> alles reden, ohne wie ein Verkäufer zu klingen.

**Bild:** Du sprichst. Ab Sekunde 12 Bildschirmaufnahme des Dashboards mit den
echten Zahlen. Kein Zoom auf Kundennamen.

**Text im Bild (Sekunde 0):** `2,715 cold emails. 1 meeting.`

**Skript**

> I sent two thousand seven hundred and fifteen cold emails.
> I got three replies and one meeting.
>
> Most people would not show you that. I build software for cold outreach, so
> that number is embarrassing for me specifically.
>
> But here is what it taught me. My problem was never volume. It was that I had
> no idea which of those emails did anything — because the tool that wrote them
> and the tool that received the replies were two different tools.
>
> So I built the thing that connects them. And the first honest number it gave
> me was this one.

**Caption**

> 2,715 sent. 1 meeting. Posting my own worst number first, because everything
> else I say about cold email should be measured against it.
>
> The lesson was not "send more". It was that nobody could tell me which email
> actually worked.

## 02 · 12 emails, 1 reply is not 8.3%

**Säule:** Zahlen, die lügen · **Länge:** 35 s · **Deine Kernaussage.**

**Bild:** Du sprichst. Bei „here is what mine does" auf die Ansicht „Nach Text"
schneiden, auf die Zeile mit `26 — too few`.

**Text im Bild:** `12 emails. 1 reply. That is not 8.3%.`

**Skript**

> Twelve emails, one reply. Your dashboard calls that eight point three percent.
>
> It is not. It is twelve emails and one reply.
>
> A percentage off twelve data points is not a rate, it is noise wearing a
> percent sign. And you are about to make a decision with it — kill this
> subject line, double down on that list.
>
> My tool refuses to show a percentage below thirty contacts. It just says
> "too few". People hate that column. It is the most useful one on the screen.

**Caption**

> Small samples do not become meaningful by adding a decimal point.
>
> Below 30 contacted people, my tool shows "too few" instead of a rate. Yes,
> that looks worse in a client report. It is also the only honest thing to put
> there.

**Hashtags:** #coldemail #b2bsales #salesops #outbound #dataliteracy

## 03 · Teardown: 164 words

**Säule:** Teardown · **Länge:** 45 s

**Bild:** Bildschirmaufnahme einer echten Mail (Absender und Firma geschwärzt).
Roter Kringel um die Textmasse. Am Ende dein Umbau daneben.

**Text im Bild:** `164 words. I stopped at word 12.`

**Skript**

> This cold email is a hundred and sixty-four words long. I stopped reading at
> word twelve.
>
> Look at where it starts. Three sentences about them — their mission, their
> journey, their commitment to excellence. That is not personalisation, that is
> throat clearing.
>
> Under ninety words is the working range for a first cold email, and the
> personalised line counts toward it.
>
> Here is the same email at sixty-one words. One observation, one offer, one
> question. Same sender, same offer. The only thing I removed was everything
> that was about them being flattered.

**Caption**

> 164 words in a first cold email. Under 90 is the working range — and your
> personalised opener counts toward it.
>
> Sender anonymised. I take apart the copy, never the person.

**Hashtags:** #coldemail #copywriting #b2bsales #outbound #salestips

## 04 · Your dashboard counts auto-replies

**Säule:** Zahlen, die lügen · **Länge:** 30 s

**Bild:** Du sprichst. Eine Abwesenheitsnotiz auf dem Bildschirm zeigen.

**Text im Bild:** `"I am out of office until the 14th." → counted as a reply.`

**Skript**

> "I am currently out of office and will return on the fourteenth."
>
> Your outreach tool just counted that as a reply. So did mine, until I looked.
>
> An autoresponder is not a person who reacted to anything. Counting it pushes
> your reply rate up — which is exactly the direction people like to be fooled
> in, so nobody checks.
>
> Go look at your last campaign and subtract them. The number you get is the
> real one, and it is the first time it means anything.

**Caption**

> Out-of-office replies are counted as replies by most outreach tools.
>
> Subtract them from your last campaign. That new, worse number is the first
> one you can actually use.

**Hashtags:** #coldemail #salesops #outbound #b2bsales #emailmarketing

## 05 · I burned a domain

**Säule:** Handwerk · **Länge:** 40 s

**Bild:** Du sprichst, durchgehend. Kein Bildschirm — das ist eine Geschichte,
keine Demonstration.

**Text im Bild:** `I burned a domain. It cost me a client.`

**Skript**

> I burned a sending domain once. It cost me a client.
>
> Here is how it goes. Bounce rate creeps past five percent. The receiving
> providers notice. And they do not forget when you fix it — the domain carries
> that reputation with it, permanently.
>
> The worst part is the timing. You do not find out on the day it happens. You
> find out three weeks later when a client asks why their reply rate collapsed.
>
> Three things would have caught it: verify every address before it enters a
> campaign, check the bounce rate mid-flight, and never, ever send from your
> main domain.

**Caption**

> Past 5% bounce rate, the receiving providers' protections kick in — and the
> domain carries that permanently.
>
> You find out three weeks later, when a client asks why replies stopped.

**Hashtags:** #coldemail #deliverability #b2bsales #outbound #emailmarketing

## 06 · Teardown: three exclamation marks

**Säule:** Teardown · **Länge:** 35 s

**Bild:** Bildschirmaufnahme. Marker auf „Free", „Act now", „!!!", „guaranteed".

**Text im Bild:** `Free. Act now. Guaranteed. !!!`

**Skript**

> Free. Act now. Guaranteed. Three exclamation marks.
>
> This is not an email, it is a spam filter test — and it failed before a human
> ever saw it.
>
> The thing is, none of these words are illegal. They are just the words that
> appear in the emails filters were trained on. You are not being punished for
> the word "free". You are being sorted into the group that uses it.
>
> Write the sentence you would say on the phone. If you would not say "act now"
> out loud to a stranger, do not put it in writing either.

**Caption**

> "Free", "act now", "guaranteed" and three exclamation marks in one cold email.
>
> None of those words are banned. They just put you in the group filters were
> trained on.

**Hashtags:** #coldemail #copywriting #deliverability #b2bsales #outbound

## 07 · Variant B tripled your reply rate

**Säule:** Zahlen, die lügen · **Länge:** 35 s

**Bild:** Ansicht „Nach Text" mit Fassung A und B nebeneinander.

**Text im Bild:** `"Variant B tripled your reply rate." (off 9 replies)`

**Skript**

> Your A/B test says variant B tripled your reply rate. It is based on nine
> replies.
>
> That is not a winner. That is a coin landing heads three times.
>
> Every dashboard I have used will happily declare that, because declaring a
> winner feels like progress and nobody complains about good news.
>
> My rule: no winner until each version has fifty sends and the gap survives a
> chance test. Most tests never get there — and knowing that is worth more than
> a fake answer.

**Caption**

> "Variant B tripled your reply rate" — off nine replies.
>
> No winner until each version has 50 sends and the gap survives a significance
> check. Most A/B tests in cold email never get there.

**Hashtags:** #abtesting #coldemail #salesops #b2bsales #dataliteracy

## 08 · Teardown: Hi {{firstName}}

**Säule:** Teardown · **Länge:** 30 s

**Bild:** Bildschirmaufnahme mit dem ungefüllten Platzhalter.

**Text im Bild:** `Hi {{firstName}},`

**Skript**

> "Hi curly brace curly brace first name."
>
> The placeholder did not even fill. But that is not the interesting failure
> here.
>
> The interesting one is that placeholder personalisation performs worse than
> no personalisation at all. Not the same — worse. Because "I saw that
> {{company}} is doing great things" tells the reader exactly one thing: you
> are number four hundred in a list.
>
> Either write one real line per contact, or write none and be honestly generic.
> The middle is the only losing option.

**Caption**

> Placeholder personalisation performs worse than no personalisation at all.
>
> "I saw that {{company}} is doing great things" tells the reader they are
> number 400 in a list.

## 09 · Two to four weeks before you send

**Säule:** Handwerk · **Länge:** 35 s

**Bild:** Du sprichst. Kurz eine Warmup-Zeitleiste einblenden.

**Text im Bild:** `Day 1: do not send.`

**Skript**

> You bought the domain, you set up the mailboxes, you are ready to send.
> Do not.
>
> Fresh mailboxes need two to four weeks of warmup. Not because a tool says so
> — because a mailbox that has never sent anything and then sends two hundred
> emails on a Tuesday looks exactly like a compromised account.
>
> This is the part everyone skips, and it is the reason most first campaigns
> land in spam and get blamed on the copy.
>
> Nothing you write in week one beats waiting until week three.

**Caption**

> Fresh mailboxes need 2–4 weeks of warmup. A mailbox that has never sent
> anything and then sends 200 emails on a Tuesday looks like a compromised
> account.
>
> Most "the copy did not work" is actually this.

**Hashtags:** #coldemail #deliverability #outbound #b2bsales #emailmarketing

## 10 · Teardown: same email, rebuilt

**Säule:** Teardown · **Länge:** 50 s · **Das stärkste Teardown-Format.**

> Endet, ohne dass jemand schlecht aussieht: es geht nicht darum, dass die Mail
> schlecht war, sondern dass dieselbe Absicht in weniger Worten trägt.

**Bild:** Geteilter Bildschirm. Links das Original, rechts dein Umbau. Beim
Sprechen die jeweilige Seite hervorheben.

**Text im Bild:** `Same offer. Same sender. 61 words instead of 164.`

**Skript**

> Same email twice. Left is how it arrived. Right is how it would have worked.
>
> I removed the introduction, because nobody cares who you are yet. I removed
> two of the three questions, because every extra question halves the chance of
> an answer. And I replaced "I saw you are doing great things" with one thing I
> could actually verify about them in ten seconds.
>
> Same offer. Same sender. Sixty-one words instead of a hundred and sixty-four.
>
> The original was not lazy. Someone spent time on it. They just spent it on
> the wrong half.

**Caption**

> Same offer, same sender, 61 words instead of 164.
>
> The original was not lazy — someone spent real time on it. They just spent it
> on the half the reader skips.
